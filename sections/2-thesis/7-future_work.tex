\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

This research demonstrates that supervised object detection and multi model foundation models each contribute different strengths to automated housing inspection. YOLOv8 achieved superior performance for detecting and localizing individual inspection categories, whereas LLaVA demonstrated stronger performance when reasoning about the overall condition of a property. Rather than viewing these approaches as competing alternatives, the findings suggest that they are complementary and operate most effectively at different levels of the inspection process.

The most important direction for future research is therefore the development of a hybrid inspection system that combines the strengths of both paradigms. In such a workflow, YOLOv8 could first identify and localize inspection relevant defects using bounding boxes. These detections could subsequently be provided as structured input to a vision language model, enabling it to reason about the overall condition of the property, generate inspection summaries, explain the detected defects, and support inspectors during decision making. Combining accurate defect localization with higher level semantic reasoning may provide more comprehensive decision support than either approach can achieve independently.

The next phase of this research will focus on implementing and evaluating such a hybrid system in collaboration with De Goede Woning. Unlike the controlled experiments presented in this thesis, the combined system will be evaluated during real housing inspections to determine whether it improves inspection efficiency, consistency between inspectors, report quality, and practical usability within existing inspection workflows.

Future work should also investigate newer multi model foundation models, including recent versions of LLaVA, Qwen2.5 VL, InternVL, and other open source vision language models. As these models continue to evolve rapidly, improvements in visual understanding and multi model reasoning may reduce the performance gap observed in this study while maintaining their flexibility and zero shot capabilities.

Another important research direction is the development of larger and more representative housing inspection datasets. Although this study harmonized multiple publicly available datasets, future benchmark datasets should contain inspection images collected from multiple housing corporations, different building types, and diverse inspection scenarios. Increasing the number of examples for underrepresented categories, particularly wear and mold, would likely improve the performance and generalizability of both supervised and multi model approaches.

Finally, future research could further investigate methodologies that have proven successful in medical imaging. Improved annotation strategies, higher image resolutions, attention based localization methods, and preprocessing techniques for enhancing subtle visual abnormalities may further improve the detection of small inspection relevant defects. Combining these methodological advances with hybrid inspection systems represents a promising direction for advancing automated housing inspection and transferring successful practices from mature computer vision domains to the housing inspection field.
