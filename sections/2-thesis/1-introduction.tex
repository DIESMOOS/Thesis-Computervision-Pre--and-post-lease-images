\section{Introduction}

Housing corporations increasingly rely on image archives to document the condition of rental properties. Inspectors compare pre and post lease images to identify damage, wear, and other inspection relevant changes. This process costs time and is partly subjective, especially when large numbers of images have to be reviewed manually. As the volume of inspection images grows, scalable and consistent decision support becomes increasingly relevant for housing corporations and inspectors.

Progress in image analysis domains, especially medical imaging, shows that AI can support the detection of subtle abnormalities. In this thesis, progress refers to improvements in detection accuracy, robustness to image variability, localization of small abnormalities, and standardized evaluation procedures. Medical imaging is relevant because models in that domain are often required to detect small and visually subtle changes under strict evaluation conditions. Housing inspection faces a similar challenge, relevant defects such as cracks, mold, wear, or surface damage may occupy only a small part of an image and may be difficult to detect under variable lighting, viewing angles, and image quality.

Deep learning has shown strong performance across visual inspection domains, including infrastructure monitoring, medical imaging, anomaly detection, and building defect detection. Within supervised object detection, YOLOv8 provides a fast, stable, and widely adopted baseline. YOLOv8 is selected in this study because it represents a mature task specific object detection framework that balances detection performance, computational efficiency, and reproducibility. Unlike foundation models, YOLOv8 produces explicit object localization through bounding boxes, which is important in inspection settings where not only the presence of damage but also its location matters.

In parallel, multi modal foundation models have risen. Models such as LLaVA 1.6 are pretrained on many image text data and can perform visual reasoning through natural language prompts without task specific training. LLaVA 1.6 is selected because it is open source, can be deployed locally, and supports controlled and reproducible experimentation. This makes it more suitable for academic comparison than proprietary vision language models, while still representing the broader class of multi modal foundation models.

Although related work shows that both supervised detectors and foundation models can detect defects or anomalies, these model families are often evaluated separately, using different datasets, metrics, and tasks. Direct comparison between task specific object detection models and multi modal foundation models under identical housing inspection conditions remain limited. As a result, their relative robustness, generalization capacity, and practical suitability for structured housing inspection are not yet fully understood.

This thesis addresses this gap by comparing YOLOv8 and LLaVA 1.6 for detecting and identifying predefined inspection relevant categories in lease housing images. By evaluating both approaches using shared datasets, shared inspection categories, and harmonized outputs, this study aims to quantify their performance, robustness, and practical usefulness for supporting housing inspection workflows.

YOLOv8 was selected because it represents a widely adopted object detection approach capable of localizing defects through bounding boxes. LLaVA 1.6 was selected as a representative open source vision language model that enables prompt based visual reasoning without task specific training. Comparing these approaches provides insight into the trade off between supervised defect localization and multi modal reasoning within housing inspection.

\subsection{Research Questions}

The main research question is:

\textbf{To what extent do task specific object detection models and multi modal foundation models differ in detecting and identifying predefined inspection relevant categories within lease housing images under real world variability?}

To answer this question, the following sub-questions are defined:

\textbf{SRQ1:} How robust are YOLOv8 and LLaVA 1.6 under variations in lighting conditions, different angles, and image quality?

\textbf{SRQ2:} How do YOLOv8 and LLaVA 1.6 compare in detecting and identifying inspection relevant categories using shared evaluation?

\textbf{SRQ3:} What advantages and limitations do YOLOv8 and LLaVA 1.6 present for practical deployment in housing inspection workflows?
